I'm Aidoudi El Fakhar Arrazi, a computer science engineer working across software development and networked systems. My current research focuses on Flying Ad-hoc Networks (FANET), using NS-3 to simulate routing protocols, mobility models, and the trade-offs that show up when nodes are fast, disconnected, and constantly moving.

Alongside research, I build software end-to-end — from small tools like Playnck to longer-running experiments — and I keep coming back to the same question in both my code and my research: how do independent parts stay coordinated when nothing holds still?

Photography is where that same attention slows down. No packets, no protocols — just light, timing, and framing. It's the other half of this site, and it's still growing.

Research

FANET & NS-3

A closer look at my primary research focus: routing and mobility in Flying Ad-hoc Networks.

Simulating resilient drone networks

Flying Ad-hoc Networks (FANETs) connect autonomous drones without fixed infrastructure — nodes join, move fast, and disconnect constantly. My work uses NS-3 to simulate this environment and measure how different routing strategies hold up under real mobility conditions.

  • Modelling drone mobility patterns and dynamic topology changes
  • Comparing routing protocols on packet delivery ratio & latency
  • Analyzing network resilience under node loss & high mobility
